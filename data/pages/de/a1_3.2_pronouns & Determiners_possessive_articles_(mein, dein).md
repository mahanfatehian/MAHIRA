# Pronouns: Possessive Articles (mein, dein) - A1

Possessive articles (*Possessivartikel*) show ownership or belonging. They answer the question "Whose?" In English, these are words like **my**, **your**, **his**, **her**, **our**, etc. [citation:3]

## Why this matters
- You use them constantly to talk about your family, your belongings, and your friends
- They change their endings based on the noun they describe (like "kein")
- Essential for introducing yourself and others naturally
- Without them, you sound like a caveman: "Where is book?" instead of "Where is **my** book?"

## The Golden Rule: Endings Match the Noun

In German, the possessive article must match the **gender** (masculine/feminine/neuter) and **case** (nominative/accusative) of the noun it describes. The ending changes – not the meaning! [citation:2][citation:3]

The good news: **Possessive articles use the same endings as the word "kein"** (no/not a). If you know those endings, you already know these! [citation:3]

## Part 1: The Possessive Stems

First, learn the base form for each "owner":

| Owner | Possessive Stem | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** (I) | **mein-** | my |
| **du** (you, informal) | **dein-** | your |
| **er** (he) | **sein-** | his |
| **sie** (she) | **ihr-** | her |
| **es** (it) | **sein-** | its |
| **wir** (we) | **unser-** | our |
| **ihr** (you guys) | **euer-** | your (plural) |
| **sie** (they) | **ihr-** | their |
| **Sie** (you, formal) | **Ihr-** | your (formal) |

**For A1, we focus on mein and dein first, then add the others gradually.** [citation:1]

## Part 2: Possessives in Nominative Case (Subject)

The **nominative case** is used when the possessed thing is the **subject** of the sentence (the thing doing the action). [citation:2]

### Ending Pattern in Nominative:

| Noun Gender | Ending | Example with **mein** | Example with **dein** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** (der) | **-e**? Wait – careful! | | |
| Let's check: | | | |

Actually, let's use a clear table:

| Noun Gender | Article | Ending | mein + ending | dein + ending |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** (der) | der Tisch | **- (no ending)** | **mein** Tisch | **dein** Tisch |
| **neuter** (das) | das Buch | **- (no ending)** | **mein** Buch | **dein** Buch |
| **feminine** (die) | die Lampe | **-e** | **meine** Lampe | **deine** Lampe |
| **plural** (die) | die Bücher | **-e** | **meine** Bücher | **deine** Bücher |

**Key rule:** In nominative, only **feminine** and **plural** nouns add an **-e** to the possessive. Masculine and neuter nouns use the base form with no ending. [citation:7]

### Examples in Nominative:

| Gender | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** | Das ist **mein Bruder**. | That is my brother. |
| | Ist das **dein Tisch**? | Is that your table? |
| **neuter** | **Mein Buch** ist neu. | My book is new. |
| | Wo ist **dein Handy**? | Where is your phone? |
| **feminine** | **Meine Mutter** heißt Anna. | My mother is called Anna. |
| | Ist das **deine Schwester**? | Is that your sister? |
| **plural** | **Meine Kinder** sind klein. | My children are small. |
| | **Deine Eltern** sind nett. | Your parents are nice. |

## Part 3: Possessives in Accusative Case (Direct Object)

The **accusative case** is used when the possessed thing is the **direct object** (the thing being acted upon). This happens with verbs like **haben**, **kaufen**, **sehen**, **essen**, etc. [citation:9]

### Ending Pattern in Accusative:

| Noun Gender | Article | Ending | mein + ending | dein + ending |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** (der) | den Tisch | **-en** | **meinen** Tisch | **deinen** Tisch |
| **neuter** (das) | das Buch | **- (no ending)** | **mein** Buch | **dein** Buch |
| **feminine** (die) | die Lampe | **-e** | **meine** Lampe | **deine** Lampe |
| **plural** (die) | die Bücher | **-e** | **meine** Bücher | **deine** Bücher |

**Key rule:** Only **masculine** nouns change in accusative – they add **-en**. Neuter, feminine, and plural stay the same as nominative. [citation:9]

### Examples in Accusative:

| Gender | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** | Ich habe **meinen Bruder** gesehen. | I saw my brother. |
| | Kaufst du **deinen Tisch**? | Are you buying your table? |
| **neuter** | Ich lese **mein Buch**. | I'm reading my book. |
| | Hast du **dein Handy**? | Do you have your phone? |
| **feminine** | Ich besuche **meine Mutter**. | I'm visiting my mother. |
| | Siehst du **deine Schwester**? | Do you see your sister? |
| **plural** | Ich liebe **meine Kinder**. | I love my children. |
| | Er kennt **deine Eltern**. | He knows your parents. |

## Part 5: Other Possessive Stems (A1)

While focusing on **mein** and **dein**, here are the other stems for reference: [citation:2][citation:7]

| Owner | Stem | Example (Nominative, masculine) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| er (he) | **sein-** | **sein** Bruder (his brother) |
| sie (she) | **ihr-** | **ihr** Bruder (her brother) |
| es (it) | **sein-** | **sein** Haus (its house) |
| wir (we) | **unser-** | **unser** Bruder (our brother) |
| ihr (you guys) | **euer-** | **euer** Bruder (your brother) |
| sie (they) | **ihr-** | **ihr** Bruder (their brother) |
| Sie (formal) | **Ihr-** | **Ihr** Bruder (your brother) |

**Important:** **euer** loses the second **-e-** when adding endings:
- euer + e = **eure** (eure Mutter)
- euer + en = **euren** (euren Vater) [citation:2]

## Part 6: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Forgetting the ending on feminine nouns ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Das ist **mein** Mutter. | Das ist **meine** Mutter. |
| Ist das **dein** Schwester? | Ist das **deine** Schwester? |

### Mistake 2: Forgetting the -en on masculine nouns in accusative ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich habe **mein** Bruder gesehen. | Ich habe **meinen** Bruder gesehen. |
| Kaufst du **dein** Tisch? | Kaufst du **deinen** Tisch? |

### Mistake 3: Using the wrong stem (confusing "her" and "their") ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Das ist **ihr** Buch. (if it means "her" – correct!) | Wait, "ihr" can mean both! Context matters: |
| **ihr** Buch = her book OR their book | You need context to know which |
| **Ihr** Buch = your book (formal) | |

### Mistake 4: Forgetting that "euer" changes form ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Habt ihr **euer** Mutter gesehen? | Habt ihr **eure** Mutter gesehen? |
| Das ist **euer** Schwester. | Das ist **eure** Schwester. |
