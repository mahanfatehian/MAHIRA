# Function Words: Basic Conjunctions (und, aber, oder) - A1

Conjunctions (*Konjunktionen*) are linking words that connect words, phrases, or sentences. The three most important conjunctions for A1 are **und** (and), **aber** (but), and **oder** (or).

## Why this matters
- You use them constantly to connect ideas and make longer sentences
- They make your German sound more natural and fluent
- They follow simple word order rules (no change!)
- Without them, you can only speak in short, choppy sentences

## The Golden Rule: No Word Order Change!

The best news about **und**, **aber**, and **oder** is that they are **coordinating conjunctions** – they **do not change the word order** . The verb stays in position 2 in both clauses, just like in a normal sentence.

| Clause 1 | Conjunction | Clause 2 (normal word order) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich lerne Deutsch, | **und** | mein Bruder lernt Englisch. |
| Ich mag Kaffee, | **aber** | mein Freund trinkt Tee. |
| Gehst du ins Kino, | **oder** | bleibst du zu Hause? |

---

## Part 1: und – and

**und** is the most common conjunction. It connects two similar ideas, adds information, or lists things.

### Connecting words:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich trinke Kaffee **und** Tee. | I drink coffee and tea. |
| Mein Bruder **und** meine Schwester sind hier. | My brother and my sister are here. |
| Er ist groß, stark **und** intelligent. | He is tall, strong and intelligent. |

### Connecting sentences:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich lerne Deutsch, **und** mein Freund lernt Englisch. | I learn German, and my friend learns English. |
| Wir gehen ins Kino, **und** danach essen wir Pizza. | We're going to the cinema, and afterwards we're eating pizza. |
| Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf, **und** dann frühstücke ich. | I get up at 7 o'clock, and then I have breakfast. |

### Special comma rule:

When **und** connects two complete sentences, you **can** put a comma before it, but you don't have to . In modern German, it's often omitted.

| With comma | Without comma |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich lerne Deutsch, **und** mein Bruder lernt Englisch. | Ich lerne Deutsch **und** mein Bruder lernt Englisch. |

When **und** connects single words or phrases, there is **no comma**:
- Ich trinke Kaffee **und** Tee. ✓
- Mein Bruder **und** meine Schwester ✓

---

## Part 2: aber – but

**aber** expresses a contrast or contradiction. It shows that the second clause is different from what you might expect from the first.

### Connecting words:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Der Film war lang, **aber** gut. | The film was long but good. |
| Sie ist klein, **aber** stark. | She is small but strong. |
| Das Essen war einfach, **aber** lecker. | The food was simple but tasty. |

### Connecting sentences:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich mag Kaffee, **aber** mein Freund trinkt Tee. | I like coffee, but my friend drinks tea. |
| Wir wollen ins Kino gehen, **aber** wir haben keine Zeit. | We want to go to the cinema, but we have no time. |
| Er ist müde, **aber** er kann nicht schlafen. | He is tired, but he can't sleep. |
| Ich habe ihn gefragt, **aber** er hat nicht geantwortet. | I asked him, but he didn't answer. |

### Position of aber:

**aber** is always at the beginning of the second clause (position 0). The verb stays in position 2.

| Clause 1 | **aber** | Position 1 | Position 2 (Verb) | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich mag Kaffee, | **aber** | mein Freund | **trinkt** | Tee. |
| Wir wollen gehen, | **aber** | wir | **haben** | keine Zeit. |

### Comma rule:

You **must** put a comma before **aber** when it connects two complete sentences .

| Correct | Wrong |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich mag Kaffee, **aber** mein Freund trinkt Tee. | Ich mag Kaffee **aber** mein Freund trinkt Tee. ❌ |

---

## Part 3: oder – or

**oder** presents an alternative or a choice between two options.

### Connecting words:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Möchtest du Tee **oder** Kaffee? | Would you like tea or coffee? |
| Kommst du heute **oder** morgen? | Are you coming today or tomorrow? |
| Rot **oder** Blau? Welche Farbe magst du? | Red or blue? Which color do you like? |

### Connecting sentences:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gehst du ins Kino, **oder** bleibst du zu Hause? | Are you going to the cinema, or are you staying home? |
| Lernst du Deutsch, **oder** lernst du Französisch? | Are you learning German, or are you learning French? |
| Wir können Pizza essen, **oder** wir können etwas anderes kochen. | We can eat pizza, or we can cook something else. |

### Questions with oder:

**oder** is very common in questions offering choices:

| Question | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Kommst du **oder** nicht? | Are you coming or not? |
| Ist das dein Buch **oder** meins? | Is that your book or mine? |
| Fahren wir mit dem Auto **oder** mit dem Zug? | Are we going by car or by train? |

### oder in statements:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Du kannst mich heute **oder** morgen besuchen. | You can visit me today or tomorrow. |
| Ich trinke nie Kaffee **oder** Tee am Abend. | I never drink coffee or tea in the evening. |

### Comma rule:

When **oder** connects two complete sentences, you **can** put a comma before it, but it's not required . Many people use it for clarity.

| With comma | Without comma |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gehst du ins Kino, **oder** bleibst du zu Hause? | Gehst du ins Kino **oder** bleibst du zu Hause? |

When **oder** connects single words or phrases, there is **no comma**:
- Tee **oder** Kaffee? ✓
- heute **oder** morgen ✓

---

## Part 4: Combined Conjunctions

You can also combine these conjunctions in longer sentences:

| Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich mag Tee **und** Kaffee, **aber** ich trinke keinen Alkohol. | I like tea and coffee, but I don't drink alcohol. |
| Kommst du heute, **oder** kommst du morgen, **oder** hast du gar keine Zeit? | Are you coming today, or are you coming tomorrow, or do you have no time at all? |
| Er ist groß, stark **und** intelligent, **aber** nicht sehr nett. | He is tall, strong and intelligent, but not very nice. |

---

## Part 5: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Putting the verb at the end after und/aber/oder ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich lerne Deutsch, **und** mein Bruder Englisch **lernt**. | Ich lerne Deutsch, **und** mein Bruder **lernt** Englisch. |
| Ich mag Kaffee, **aber** mein Freund Tee **trinkt**. | Ich mag Kaffee, **aber** mein Freund **trinkt** Tee. |

### Mistake 2: Forgetting the comma before aber ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich mag Kaffee **aber** mein Freund trinkt Tee. | Ich mag Kaffee, **aber** mein Freund trinkt Tee. |

### Mistake 3: Using "und" too much (like children) ❌
Instead of: Ich bin aufgestanden **und** habe gefrühstückt **und** bin zur Arbeit gegangen **und** habe gearbeitet...
Try to vary your sentences, but at A1 level, it's okay!

### Mistake 4: Confusing "aber" and "sondern" ❌

**Important:** At A1 level, you only need **aber**. But for future reference:
- **aber** = but (general contrast)
- **sondern** = but rather (after a negative)

Example where you'll need **sondern** later:
- Ich trinke **nicht** Kaffee, **sondern** Tee. (I don't drink coffee, but rather tea.)

For A1, stick with **aber** for everything!

### Part 2: Connect the sentences
Use **und**, **aber**, or **oder** to connect these sentences.

1) Ich lerne Deutsch. Mein Bruder lernt Englisch. (and)
2) Ich mag Pizza. Ich mag keine Spaghetti. (but)
3) Gehst du ins Kino? Bleibst du zu Hause? (or)
4) Wir haben Zeit. Wir gehen spazieren. (and)
5) Er will kommen. Er hat keine Zeit. (but)
6) Trinkst du Kaffee? Trinkst du Tee? (or)

### Part 3: Correct the mistakes
These sentences have mistakes. Fix them.

1) Ich lerne Deutsch und mein Bruder Englisch lernt.
2) Ich mag Kaffee aber mein Freund trinkt Tee.
3) Kommst du heute oder du kommst morgen? (careful with word order!)
4) Wir gehen ins Kino und dann essen wir Pizza. (this is actually correct! Good job)
5) Ich habe einen Bruder, und eine Schwester. (comma unnecessary)

1) I like tea and coffee.
2) Do you want tea or coffee?

5) Are you coming today or tomorrow?
6) He is tired, but he can't sleep.
