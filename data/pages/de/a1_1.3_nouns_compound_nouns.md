# Nouns: Compound Nouns (A1)

German is famous for its long words! These are often **compound nouns** (*Komposita*) – new nouns made by combining two or more words.

## Why this matters
- It's how German builds specific vocabulary efficiently
- Once you understand the rules, you can understand and even create new words
- Helps decode long texts without always needing a dictionary
- Very common in everyday language (newspapers, signs, instructions)

## The Golden Rule: Last Word Wins!
In a compound noun, **the LAST word determines everything**:
- The **gender** (der/die/das comes from the last noun)
- The **plural form** (the last noun's plural rule applies)
- The **core meaning** (the last noun is the base thing)

The words before it describe or specify the last word in more detail.

### Examples:
- **die Sonne** (sun, feminine) + **die Brille** (glasses, feminine) = **die Sonnenbrille** (sunglasses - still feminine)
- **der Tisch** (table, masculine) + **das Bein** (leg, neuter) = **das Tischbein** (table leg - now neuter!)
- **das Auto** (car, neuter) + **der Unfall** (accident, masculine) = **der Autounfall** (car accident - now masculine!)

## Building Blocks: What Can You Combine?

### 1. Noun + Noun (Most Common)
| First Word | Last Word | Compound Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| die Sonne (sun) | der Schirm (umbrella) | der Sonnenschirm (sun umbrella/parasol) |
| das Kind (child) | das Zimmer (room) | das Kinderzimmer (children's room) |
| der Bus (bus) | der Fahrer (driver) | der Busfahrer (bus driver) |
| die Schule (school) | das Buch (book) | das Schulbuch (school book) |

### 2. Adjective + Noun
| First Word | Last Word | Compound Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| groß (big/large) | die Stadt (city) | die Großstadt (city/metropolis) |
| kalt (cold) | der Schrank (cupboard) | der Kühlschrank (refrigerator - literally "cold cupboard") |
| hoch (high) | das Haus (house) | das Hochhaus (high-rise building) |
| schnell (fast) | der Zug (train) | der Schnellzug (express train) |

### 3. Verb Stem + Noun
The verb loses its -en ending and just the stem is used.

| Verb | Last Word | Compound Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| schlafen (to sleep) | das Zimmer (room) | das Schlafzimmer (bedroom) |
| essen (to eat) | das Zimmer (room) | das Esszimmer (dining room) |
| trinken (to drink) | das Wasser (water) | das Trinkwasser (drinking water) |
| fahren (to drive) | das Rad (wheel) | das Fahrrad (bicycle) |

### 4. Preposition + Noun
| First Word | Last Word | Compound Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| vor (in front of/before) | der Ort (place) | der Vorort (suburb) |
| neben (next to) | die Sache (thing) | die Nebensache (minor matter) |
| nach (after) | der Tisch (table) | der Nachtisch (dessert - literally "after-table") |

## Connecting Elements (Fugenelemente)
Sometimes a little extra letter is added between words to make pronunciation smoother. These are called **Fugenlaute** (connecting sounds) [citation:2][citation:5].

| Connector | Example | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **-s-** | das Liebe**s**lied (love song) | das Lied (song) + die Liebe (love) |
| **-n- / -en-** | die Sonne**n**brille (sunglasses) | die Brille + die Sonne |
| **-e-** | der Hunde**e**leine (dog leash) | die Leine + der Hund |
| **-er-** | das Kinder**er**zimmer (children's room) | das Zimmer + das Kind |
| **-ens-** | der Schmerze**ns**geld (compensation) | das Geld + der Schmerz |

There are no fixed rules for when to use these – you learn them through exposure.

## Fun with Long Words
Germans love creating descriptive compounds. Here are some real (and fun) examples:

| Compound Noun | Breakdown | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| der Handschuh | die Hand (hand) + der Schuh (shoe) | glove ("hand shoe") |
| der Staubsauger | der Staub (dust) + saugen (to suck) | vacuum cleaner ("dust sucker") |
| die Nacktschnecke | nackt (naked) + die Schnecke (snail) | slug ("naked snail") |
| der Fußbodenschleifmaschinenverleih | der Fußboden (floor) + schleifen (to sand) + die Maschine (machine) + der Verleih (rental) | floor sanding machine rental |
