# Pronouns: Demonstrative Pronouns (dieser, jener, der) - A1

Demonstrative pronouns (*Demonstrativpronomen*) point to specific people or things. They are used to say "this one" or "that one" in German. They are stronger and more precise than the definite article (der/die/das) [citation:5].

## Why this matters
- You use them to point out specific things: "**This** book is mine, not **that** one."
- They help avoid confusion when referring back to something already mentioned
- Essential for shopping, choosing between options, and clarifying
- Very common in everyday conversations [citation:2]

## The Golden Rule: Endings Match the Noun

Like possessive articles, demonstrative pronouns change their endings based on the **gender** (masculine/feminine/neuter), **number** (singular/plural), and **case** (nominative/accusative) of the noun they replace or describe.

The good news: Most demonstratives follow the same pattern as the definite article (der/die/das)!

---

## Part 1: dieser/diese/dieses – "this/these"

**dieser** is used for things that are **near** in space or time [citation:5][citation:7]. It means "this" (singular) or "these" (plural).

### Declension of dieser in Nominative and Accusative

| Case | Masculine | Neuter | Feminine | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | **dieser** Mann | **dieses** Kind | **diese** Frau | **diese** Leute |
| **Accusative** | **diesen** Mann | **dieses** Kind | **diese** Frau | **diese** Leute |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dieser** Tisch ist neu. | This table is new. |
| **Dieses** Buch gehört mir. | This book belongs to me. |
| **Diese** Lampe ist kaputt. | This lamp is broken. |
| Ich kaufe **diesen** Stuhl. | I'm buying this chair. (accusative, masculine) |
| Magst du **dieses** Auto? | Do you like this car? |
| **Diese** Schuhe sind zu klein. | These shoes are too small. |

---

## Part 2: jener/jene/jenes – "that/those"

**jener** is used for things that are **far** in space or time [citation:7]. It means "that" (singular) or "those" (plural). 

**Note for A1:** *jener* is less common in spoken German today. Native speakers often use *der da* or *das da* instead. But you should recognize it!

### Declension of jener in Nominative and Accusative

| Case | Masculine | Neuter | Feminine | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | **jener** Mann | **jenes** Kind | **jene** Frau | **jene** Leute |
| **Accusative** | **jenen** Mann | **jenes** Kind | **jene** Frau | **jene** Leute |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Jener** Baum dort ist alt. | That tree over there is old. |
| **Jenes** Haus gehört meinem Onkel. | That house belongs to my uncle. |
| Kennst du **jene** Frau? | Do you know that woman? |
| Ich möchte **jenen** Schrank kaufen. | I want to buy that cupboard. |

### Modern Alternative:
Instead of *jener*, Germans often say:

| Modern | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Der** Mann **da** | That man there |
| **Das** Haus **da drüben** | That house over there |
| **Die** Frau **dort** | That woman there |

## Part 3: der/die/das as Demonstrative Pronouns

The definite article can also be used as a **demonstrative pronoun** meaning "that one" or "this one" [citation:1][citation:4]. When used this way, it is STRESSED in speech (pronounced more strongly).

### Comparison: Article vs. Demonstrative

| As Article (weak) | As Demonstrative (strong) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Der** Mann ist groß. (The man is tall.) | **Der** ist groß. (That one is tall.) |
| Ich mag **die** Frau. (I like the woman.) | Ich mag **die**. (I like her/that one.) |
| Hast du **das** Buch? (Do you have the book?) | Hast du **das**? (Do you have that?) |

### Declension of der/die/das as Demonstrative

| Case | Masculine | Neuter | Feminine | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | **der** | **das** | **die** | **die** |
| **Accusative** | **den** | **das** | **die** | **die** |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich suche meinen Stift. **Den** finde ich nicht. | I'm looking for my pen. I can't find **it**. | "Den" refers to the pen |
| Magst du den Kuchen? Ja, **den** finde ich lecker. | Do you like the cake? Yes, **that one** is tasty. | |
| Kennst du Maria? Ja, **die** ist meine Freundin. | Do you know Maria? Yes, **she** is my friend. | |
| Hast du mein Buch gesehen? **Das** liegt auf dem Tisch. | Have you seen my book? **It** is on the table. | |

## Part 4: derselbe/dieselbe/dasselbe – "the same"

These compound demonstratives mean "the same" [citation:4]. They combine **der/die/das** + **selbe**.

### Declension

| Case | Masculine | Neuter | Feminine | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | **derselbe** | **dasselbe** | **dieselbe** | **dieselben** |
| **Accusative** | **denselben** | **dasselbe** | **dieselbe** | **dieselben** |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wir wohnen in **demselben** Haus. | We live in the same house. |
| Sie trägt **dasselbe** Kleid wie ich. | She's wearing the same dress as me. |
| Ich habe **denselben** Fehler gemacht. | I made the same mistake. |

---

## Part 5: Contrast – dieser vs. jener

| dieser (this/near) | jener (that/far) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Nähe (close) | Ferne (far) |
| jetzt (now) | damals (back then) |
| hier (here) | dort (there) |

### Example Contrast:

> **Dieser** Hund hier ist klein, aber **jener** Hund dort ist groß.
> (This dog here is small, but that dog there is big.)

## Part 6: Summary Table – Nominative & Accusative

| Pronoun | Meaning | Nominative | Accusative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **dieser** | this (masc) | dieser | **diesen** |
| **dieses** | this (neut) | dieses | dieses |
| **diese** | this (fem) / these (pl) | diese | diese |
| **jener** | that (masc) | jener | **jenen** |
| **jenes** | that (neut) | jenes | jenes |
| **jene** | that (fem) / those (pl) | jene | jene |
| **der** | that one (masc) | der | **den** |
| **das** | that one (neut) | das | das |
| **die** | that one (fem) / those (pl) | die | die |
