# Sentence Structure: W-Questions (W-Fragen) - A1

W-questions are questions that begin with a question word starting with "W" in German. They ask for specific information, not just "yes" or "no". These are the most common questions you'll use when getting to know people and navigating daily life.

## Why this matters
- You need them for every basic conversation: asking names, locations, times, reasons
- They follow a strict and simple word order
- Essential for the Goethe A1 exam (listening and speaking sections)
- Without them, you can only get yes/no answers – very limiting!

## The Golden Rule: W-Word First, Verb Second!

In German W-questions, the **W-word** is in position 1, the **verb** is in position 2, and the **subject** follows in position 3 .

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Rest of Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **W-Word** | **VERB** | **Subject** | ... |
| Wo | wohnst | du? | |
| Wann | kommst | du | nach Hause? |
| Was | machst | du | heute? |
| Wie | heißt | dein Bruder? | |

**Common mistake to avoid:** ❌ Wann du kommst? → ✅ Wann **kommst du**?

## Part 1: The Most Important W-Questions for A1

### 1. **Wer?** – Who? (Asking about people)

Use **wer** when asking about the subject of the sentence (the person doing the action). It's for people, not things.

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wer** ist das? | Das ist mein Bruder. | Who is that? |
| **Wer** kommt heute? | Anna kommt heute. | Who is coming today? |
| **Wer** hat mein Buch? | Ich habe es. | Who has my book? |
| **Wer** ist dein Lehrer? | Herr Müller ist mein Lehrer. | Who is your teacher? |

**Grammar note:** At A1 level, you'll mostly use **wer** in the nominative case (as the subject). For "whom" (accusative: **wen**) and "to whom" (dative: **wem**), you'll learn those later .

---

### 2. **Was?** – What? (Asking about things)

Use **was** for objects, activities, ideas, and abstract concepts.

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Was** ist das? | Das ist ein Buch. | What is that? |
| **Was** machst du? | Ich lerne Deutsch. | What are you doing? |
| **Was** isst du gern? | Ich esse gern Pizza. | What do you like to eat? |
| **Was** kostet das? | Das kostet 10 Euro. | What does that cost? |
| **Was** ist dein Hobby? | Mein Hobby ist Fußball. | What is your hobby? |

---

### 3. **Wo?** – Where? (Location)

Use **wo** when asking about a static location – where something is or where something happens.

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wo** wohnst du? | Ich wohne in Berlin. | Where do you live? |
| **Wo** ist der Bahnhof? | Der Bahnhof ist dort. | Where is the train station? |
| **Wo** arbeitest du? | Ich arbeite im Büro. | Where do you work? |
| **Wo** bist du? | Ich bin zu Hause. | Where are you? |
| **Wo** ist die Toilette? | Gleich da hinten. | Where is the toilet? |

---

### 4. **Woher?** – Where from? (Origin/Source)

Use **woher** to ask where someone or something comes from. Notice the **her** indicates movement towards the speaker .

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Woher** kommst du? | Ich komme aus Spanien. | Where do you come from? |
| **Woher** hast du das Buch? | Aus der Bibliothek. | Where did you get the book? |
| **Woher** weißt du das? | Von meiner Schwester. | How do you know that? (lit. Where from do you know that?) |

**Tip:** In answers, use **aus** + country/city (aus Spanien) or **von** + person (von meiner Mutter).

---

### 5. **Wohin?** – Where to? (Direction)

Use **wohin** to ask about movement towards a destination .

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wohin** gehst du? | Ich gehe ins Kino. | Where are you going (to)? |
| **Wohin** fährst du im Urlaub? | Nach Italien. | Where are you traveling to on vacation? |
| **Wohin** soll ich das legen? | Auf den Tisch. | Where should I put this? |

**Important difference:**
- **wo** = location (static) – Wo bist du? (Where are you?)
- **wohin** = direction (movement towards) – Wohin gehst du? (Where are you going?)

---

### 6. **Wann?** – When? (Time)

Use **wann** to ask about specific times, days, or dates .

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wann** kommst du? | Um 8 Uhr. | When are you coming? |
| **Wann** hast du Geburtstag? | Am 15. Mai. | When is your birthday? |
| **Wann** beginnt der Film? | Um 20 Uhr. | When does the film start? |
| **Wann** hast du Zeit? | Morgen Nachmittag. | When do you have time? |
| **Wann** gehst du schlafen? | Gegen 22 Uhr. | When do you go to sleep? |

---

### 7. **Wie?** – How? (Manner/Way)

Use **wie** to ask about the way something is done, or as part of many common combinations .

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wie** heißt du? | Ich heiße Maria. | What is your name? (lit. How are you called?) |
| **Wie** geht es dir? | Gut, danke. | How are you? |
| **Wie** komme ich zum Bahnhof? | Gehen Sie geradeaus. | How do I get to the station? |
| **Wie** ist das Wetter? | Es ist sonnig. | How is the weather? |
| **Wie** findest du Berlin? | Ich finde es toll. | How do you like Berlin? |

---

### 8. **Wie + Adjective** – Asking for specific details

These combinations are extremely common at A1 level :

| Question | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wie alt** | how old | **Wie alt** bist du? | How old are you? |
| **Wie groß** | how tall | **Wie groß** ist dein Bruder? | How tall is your brother? |
| **Wie lang** | how long | **Wie lang** ist der Tisch? | How long is the table? |
| **Wie oft** | how often | **Wie oft** gehst du ins Kino? | How often do you go to the cinema? |
| **Wie viel** | how much | **Wie viel** kostet das? | How much does that cost? |
| **Wie viele** | how many | **Wie viele** Geschwister hast du? | How many siblings do you have? |
| **Wie lange** | how long (time) | **Wie lange** lernst du Deutsch? | How long have you been learning German? |
| **Wie weit** | how far | **Wie weit** ist es bis Berlin? | How far is it to Berlin? |

---

### 9. **Warum?** – Why? (Reason)

Use **warum** to ask for a reason. The answer often uses **weil** (because) .

| Question | Example Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warum** lernst du Deutsch? | Weil es Spaß macht. | Why are you learning German? |
| **Warum** kommst du nicht? | Ich habe keine Zeit. | Why aren't you coming? |
| **Warum** ist der Himmel blau? | Das weiß ich nicht. | Why is the sky blue? |
| **Warum** bist du müde? | Ich habe schlecht geschlafen. | Why are you tired? |

---

### 10. **Welch-?** – Which? (Selection)

Use **welcher/welche/welches** when choosing from specific options. The ending matches the gender and case of the noun .

| Gender | Nominative | Accusative | Example Question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| masculine (der) | **Welcher** | **Welchen** | **Welcher** Bus fährt nach Berlin? |
| feminine (die) | **Welche** | **Welche** | **Welche** Übung machen wir jetzt? |
| neuter (das) | **Welches** | **Welches** | **Welches** Buch liest du? |
| plural (die) | **Welche** | **Welche** | **Welche** Filme magst du? |

### Examples:

| Question | Answer | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Welcher** Tisch ist deiner? | Der braune Tisch. | Which table is yours? |
| **Welche** Lampe kaufst du? | Die rote Lampe. | Which lamp are you buying? |
| **Welches** Auto fährst du? | Einen BMW. | Which car do you drive? |
| **Welche** Sprachen sprichst du? | Deutsch und Englisch. | Which languages do you speak? |

## Part 2: Word Order in W-Questions – Detailed Breakdown

### Basic Structure:

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 4 | Position 5 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **W-Word** | **VERB** | **Subject** | **Time** | **Object/Place** |
| Wo | wohnst | du? | | |
| Wann | kommst | du | heute? | |
| Was | machst | du | heute | Abend? |
| Warum | lernst | du | | Deutsch? |
| Wie | heißt | deine Mutter? | | |

### With modal verbs (modal in position 2, main verb at the end):

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Middle | End (Infinitive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wann | **kannst** | du | mich | **besuchen**? |
| Was | **willst** | du | heute Abend | **essen**? |
| Wo | **möchtest** | du | | **wohnen**? |
| Warum | **musst** | du | so früh | **gehen**? |

### With separable verbs (prefix goes to the end):

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Middle | End (Prefix) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wann | **stehst** | du | morgen | **auf**? |
| Wen | **rufst** | du | später | **an**? |
| Wo | **kaufst** | du | ein? | |

## Part 3: W-Questions vs. Yes/No Questions

| Question Type | Position 1 | Position 2 | Answer Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yes/No Question** | **VERB** | Subject | Ja / Nein |
| | **Lernst** | du Deutsch? | Yes/No |
| **W-Question** | **W-Word** | Verb | Specific information |
| | **Was** | lernst | du? | "Deutsch" |

### Compare:

| Question | Expected Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lernst** du Deutsch? | Ja / Nein |
| **Was** lernst du? | Deutsch. (specific information) |
| **Kommst** du heute? | Ja / Nein |
| **Wann** kommst du? | Um 8 Uhr. (specific time) |
| **Ist** das dein Bruder? | Ja / Nein |
| **Wer** ist das? | Mein Bruder. (specific person) |

## Part 4: The Formal "Sie" in W-Questions

When using the formal "Sie", the verb conjugation is plural (same as "sie" – they), and "Sie" is capitalized .

| Informal (du) | Formal (Sie) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wo **wohnst** **du**? | Wo **wohnen** **Sie**? |
| Wie **heißt** **du**? | Wie **heißen** **Sie**? |
| Wann **kommst** **du**? | Wann **kommen** **Sie**? |
| Was **machst** **du**? | Was **machen** **Sie**? |
| Haben **Sie** Kinder? (Question with haben – note: this is a Yes/No question, but the pattern is the same for formal Sie) |

### Examples in context:

| Situation | Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Talking to a friend | **Woher** **kommst** **du**? |
| Talking to your boss | **Woher** **kommen** **Sie**? |
| Talking to a child | **Wie** **heißt** **du**? |
| Talking to a stranger | **Wie** **heißen** **Sie**? |

## Part 5: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Putting the verb at the end ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wo du **wohnst**? | Wo **wohnst** du? |
| Wann du **kommst**? | Wann **kommst** du? |
| Was du **machst**? | Was **machst** du? |

### Mistake 2: Forgetting the subject after the verb ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wann kommt **nach Hause**? (missing subject) | Wann **kommst du** nach Hause? |

### Mistake 3: Using the wrong W-word ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wo** gehst du? (if asking about destination) | **Wohin** gehst du? |
| **Wer** ist das? (pointing at an object) | **Was** ist das? |
| **Wie** Uhr ist es? | **Wie viel** Uhr ist es? / **Wieviel** Uhr? |

### Mistake 4: Forgetting the separable prefix at the end ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wann **aufstehst** du? | Wann **stehst** du **auf**? |
| Wen **anrufst** du? | Wen **rufst** du **an**? |

### Mistake 5: Confusing "wie viel" and "wie viele" ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wie viel** Geschwister hast du? | **Wie viele** Geschwister hast du? (countable) |
| **Wie viele** kostet das? | **Wie viel** kostet das? (uncountable/money) |
