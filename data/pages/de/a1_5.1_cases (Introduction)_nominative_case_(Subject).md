# Cases: Introduction (Die Fälle) - A1

In German, nouns and pronouns change depending on their **function** in the sentence. These different functions are called **cases** (*Fälle* or *Kasus*).

## Why this matters
- Cases tell you **who is doing what to whom** in a sentence
- They affect articles (der → den), pronouns (ich → mich), and adjective endings
- Without cases, sentences become ambiguous and confusing
- English uses word order to show these relationships; German uses cases

## The Four German Cases

German has four cases. At A1 level, you need to master the first two completely:

| Case | German Name | Function | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Nominative** | *Wer-Fall* | The **subject** (the doer) | **Der Mann** schläft. |
| **2. Accusative** | *Wen-Fall* | The **direct object** (the receiver) | Ich sehe **den Mann**. |
| **3. Dative** | *Wem-Fall* | The **indirect object** (the beneficiary) | Ich helfe **dem Mann**. |
| **4. Genitive** | *Wes-Fall* | Showing **possession** | Das Buch **des Mannes**. |

**For A1, focus on Nominative and Accusative.** Dative appears in fixed expressions, and Genitive is barely touched until A2.

# Nominative Case (Subject) - A1

The **nominative case** is the basic form of nouns and pronouns. It answers the question: **"Who?"** or **"What?"** is doing the action.

## Why this matters
- Every sentence has a subject in nominative
- The verb **agrees** with the subject (ich **lerne**, du **lernst**)
- You use it constantly: introducing people, describing things, talking about yourself
- It's the "dictionary form" of nouns (der, die, das)

## The Golden Rule: The Subject is Always Nominative!

The **subject** of a sentence – the person or thing doing the action – is always in the nominative case .

| Question | Answer (Subject in Nominative) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wer** schläft? (Who is sleeping?) | **Der Mann** schläft. |
| **Was** ist das? (What is that?) | Das ist **ein Buch**. |
| **Wer** kommt? (Who is coming?) | **Ich** komme. |

---

## Part 1: Nominative with Articles

In nominative, the articles have their **basic form**:

| Gender | Definite Article (the) | Indefinite Article (a/an) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** (der) | **der** Mann | **ein** Mann |
| **feminine** (die) | **die** Frau | **eine** Frau |
| **neuter** (das) | **das** Kind | **ein** Kind |
| **plural** (die) | **die** Männer | **(no indefinite article)** |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Der Mann** ist müde. | The man is tired. |
| **Die Frau** heißt Anna. | The woman is called Anna. |
| **Das Kind** spielt. | The child is playing. |
| **Ein Mann** kommt. | A man is coming. |
| **Eine Frau** arbeitet hier. | A woman works here. |
| **Ein Kind** weint. | A child is crying. |

---

## Part 2: The Verb "sein" – Nominative on Both Sides!

With the verb **sein** (to be), both the subject and the noun after it are in the **nominative case** .

| Subject (Nominative) | sein | Predicate Noun (also Nominative!) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ich** | bin | **ein Student**. |
| **Das** | ist | **ein Tisch**. |
| **Mein Bruder** | ist | **Lehrer**. (no article with professions!) |
| **Sie** | ist | **eine gute Freundin**. |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Das** ist **ein Buch**. | That is a book. |
| **Er** ist **Arzt**. | He is a doctor. |
| **Meine Mutter** ist **Lehrerin**. | My mother is a teacher. |
| **Das** sind **meine Eltern**. | Those are my parents. |

**Important:** With professions, nationalities, and religions, you often use **no article** in German:

| English | German |
| :--- | :--- |
| He is **a** teacher. | Er ist **–** Lehrer. |
| She is **a** doctor. | Sie ist **–** Ärztin. |
| I am **a** student. | Ich bin **–** Student. |

---

## Part 3: Nominative with Possessive Articles

Possessive articles (mein, dein, etc.) also change based on gender – but only in feminine and plural!

| Gender | Ending | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **masculine** | **-** (no ending) | **mein** Bruder |
| **neuter** | **-** (no ending) | **mein** Buch |
| **feminine** | **-e** | **meine** Schwester |
| **plural** | **-e** | **meine** Kinder |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mein Vater** arbeitet in Berlin. | My father works in Berlin. |
| **Deine Mutter** ist nett. | Your mother is nice. |
| **Sein Haus** ist groß. | His house is big. |
| **Ihre Kinder** spielen draußen. | Her/Their children are playing outside. |

---

## Part 4: Personal Pronouns in Nominative

The nominative pronouns are the ones you use as the subject:

| Person | Pronoun | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st singular | **ich** | I |
| 2nd singular (informal) | **du** | you |
| 3rd singular masculine | **er** | he |
| 3rd singular feminine | **sie** | she |
| 3rd singular neuter | **es** | it |
| 1st plural | **wir** | we |
| 2nd plural (informal) | **ihr** | you guys |
| 3rd plural | **sie** | they |
| Formal (singular/plural) | **Sie** | you (formal) |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ich** lerne Deutsch. | I learn German. |
| **Du** bist mein Freund. | You are my friend. |
| **Er** heißt Paul. | He is called Paul. |
| **Sie** ist meine Schwester. | She is my sister. |
| **Es** regnet. | It's raining. |
| **Wir** gehen ins Kino. | We're going to the cinema. |
| **Ihr** seid nett. | You guys are nice. |
| **Sie** wohnen in Berlin. | They live in Berlin. |
| **Sie** (formal), wo wohnen **Sie**? | You (formal), where do you live? |

---

## Part 5: Asking for the Subject – wer?

To ask about the subject (a person), use **wer** (who) .

| Question | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wer** ist das? | Das ist **mein Bruder**. |
| **Wer** kommt heute? | **Meine Freundin** kommt. |
| **Wer** hat mein Buch? | **Ich** habe es. |

To ask about the subject (a thing), use **was** (what) .

| Question | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Was** ist das? | Das ist **ein Buch**. |
| **Was** liegt auf dem Tisch? | **Ein Schlüssel** liegt da. |

---

## Part 6: The Nominative in Sentence Structure

In a statement, the subject (nominative) is often in **position 1**, but it can also be in **position 3** if something else starts the sentence .

| Position 1 | Position 2 (VERB) | Position 3 (Subject) | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ich** | lerne | – | Deutsch. |
| **Mein Bruder** | kommt | – | heute. |
| **Heute** | kommt | **mein Bruder**. | |
| **Im Kino** | sehen | **wir** | einen Film. |

**Key point:** The subject is always in nominative, no matter where it appears in the sentence!

---

## Summary: Nominative Case – When to Use It

Use the nominative case for:

| Use | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The subject** of the sentence | **Der Hund** bellt. |
| After the verb **sein** (to be) | Das ist **ein Tisch**. |
| After the verb **werden** (to become) | Er wird **Lehrer**. |
| After the verb **bleiben** (to stay) | Sie bleibt **meine Freundin**. |
| In **titles and headings** | **Der Spiegel** (the magazine) |
| When **addressing someone** (vocative) | **Kinder**, seid leise! |
