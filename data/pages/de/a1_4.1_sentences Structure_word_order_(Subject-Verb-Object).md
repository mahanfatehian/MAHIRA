# Sentence Structure: Word Order (Subject-Verb-Object) - A1

German word order is one of the most important things to learn from the beginning. Unlike English, where word order is relatively fixed, German has more flexibility – but with one **golden rule** you must always follow.

## Why this matters
- Every sentence you build must follow this structure
- Getting word order wrong makes you very hard to understand
- It's the foundation for all more complex sentences later
- German speakers rely on these patterns to understand meaning

## The Golden Rule: The Verb is ALWAYS in Position 2!

In a German main clause (*Hauptsatz*), the conjugated verb is always the **second idea** . This is the most important rule in all of German grammar!

| Position 1 | Position 2 (VERB!) | Rest of Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich | **lerne** | Deutsch. |
| Du | **kommst** | aus Berlin. |
| Wir | **gehen** | ins Kino. |

**Important:** "Position 2" means the second *idea* or *element*, not necessarily the second word. A single element can be multiple words.

---

## Part 1: Basic Word Order (Subject – Verb – Object)

The simplest and most common structure is exactly like English:

> **Subject – Verb – Object**

| Subject (Position 1) | Verb (Position 2) | Object (Rest) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich | **lerne** | Deutsch. |
| Der Mann | **kauft** | ein Auto. |
| Die Kinder | **spielen** | Fußball. |
| Meine Mutter | **kocht** | Suppe. |

### Examples with analysis:

| Sentence | Translation | Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich **esse** einen Apfel. | I eat an apple. | Subject – Verb – Object |
| Du **hast** einen Bruder. | You have a brother. | Subject – Verb – Object |
| Wir **trinken** Kaffee. | We drink coffee. | Subject – Verb – Object |

---

## Part 2: The Verb is ALWAYS Second!

This is the rule that never breaks. Even when you start the sentence with something other than the subject, the verb still stays in position 2.

### Examples with different starting elements:

| Position 1 (any element) | Position 2 (VERB!) | Rest of Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heute** | **lerne** | ich Deutsch. |
| **Im Kino** | **sehen** | wir einen Film. |
| **Morgen** | **fahre** | ich nach Berlin. |
| **Aus Spanien** | **kommt** | mein Freund. |
| **Den Kuchen** | **mag** | ich sehr. |

### Compare:

| English word order | German word order |
| :--- | :--- |
| Today **I learn** German. | Today **learn I** German. |
| Tomorrow **I go** to Berlin. | Tomorrow **go I** to Berlin. |
| In the cinema **we watch** a film. | In the cinema **watch we** a film. |

**Key insight:** In English, the subject comes before the verb. In German, the verb comes SECOND, no matter what comes first!

---

## Part 3: The "Kick" – Verb Pushes Subject to Position 3

When you put something other than the subject in position 1, the **subject moves to position 3** (immediately after the verb).

### Structure:

| Position 1 | Position 2 (VERB) | Position 3 (Subject) | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Time | Verb | Subject | Object/Place |
| Heute | **lerne** | **ich** | Deutsch. |
| Morgen | **fahre** | **mein Bruder** | nach Berlin. |
| Im Park | **spielen** | **die Kinder** | Fußball. |

### More examples:

| Sentence | Word by word translation | Natural translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Heute **lernen wir** Deutsch. | Today learn we German. | Today we learn German. |
| Morgen **kommt mein Freund**. | Tomorrow comes my friend. | Tomorrow my friend is coming. |
| Im Sommer **reisen wir** nach Italien. | In summer travel we to Italy. | In summer we travel to Italy. |

---

## Part 4: Time – Manner – Place (TMP)

When you have multiple details in a sentence, German follows a specific order: **Time – Manner – Place** .

| Order | What | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Time** (wann?) | heute / morgen / um 8 Uhr |
| 2 | **Manner** (wie?) | mit dem Bus / schnell / gern |
| 3 | **Place** (wo?/wohin?) | nach Berlin / im Park / nach Hause |

### Examples:

| Sentence | Time | Manner | Place |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ich fahre **morgen mit dem Bus nach Berlin**. | morgen | mit dem Bus | nach Berlin |
| Wir gehen **heute Abend ins Kino**. | heute Abend | – | ins Kino |
| Sie kommt **um 8 Uhr mit dem Zug in Köln an**. | um 8 Uhr | mit dem Zug | in Köln |

### Full sentence breakdown:

> Ich fahre **morgen** **mit dem Bus** **nach Berlin**.
> (I travel tomorrow by bus to Berlin.)

> Wir essen **heute Abend** **im Restaurant** **Pizza**.
> (We eat this evening in the restaurant pizza.) – Wait, object comes after TMP? Let's check:

Actually, the TMP rule applies to **adverbs and prepositional phrases**. The **object** usually comes after place, or sometimes before manner. At A1 level, a simpler rule is:

**Subject – Verb – Time – Object – Manner – Place**

But that's getting complex. For A1, focus on: **Time before Place** is the most important part.

## Part 5: Position of "nicht" (negation)

The word **nicht** (not) usually comes at the end of the sentence, but before specific elements you want to negate.

### Basic rule: **nicht** at the end

| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich lerne Deutsch **nicht**. | I don't learn German. |
| Er kommt heute **nicht**. | He's not coming today. |
| Wir gehen ins Kino **nicht**. | We're not going to the cinema. |

### Rule for negating specific elements: **nicht** comes BEFORE that element

| Negating | Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a specific word | Ich lerne **nicht** Deutsch, sondern Englisch. | I don't learn German, but English. |
| a time phrase | Er kommt **nicht** heute, sondern morgen. | He's not coming today, but tomorrow. |
| a place | Wir gehen **nicht** ins Kino, sondern ins Theater. | We're not going to the cinema, but to the theater. |

## Part 6: Questions – Two Types

### Type 1: Yes/No Questions (Verb in Position 1)

| Verb (Position 1) | Subject (Position 2) | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lernst** | du | Deutsch? |
| **Kommst** | du | heute? |
| **Gehen** | wir | ins Kino? |
| **Hast** | du | einen Bruder? |

### Type 2: W-Questions (W-Word in Position 1, Verb in Position 2)

| W-Word (Position 1) | Verb (Position 2) | Subject (Position 3) | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wo** | **wohnst** | du? | |
| **Wann** | **kommst** | du | nach Hause? |
| **Was** | **machst** | du | heute? |
| **Wie** | **heißt** | dein Bruder? | |

## Part 7: Summary Table – Word Order Patterns

| Sentence Type | Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Rest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Statement** | Subject | **VERB** | (Object) | Time – Place |
| | Ich | **lerne** | Deutsch | heute. |
| **Statement (time first)** | Time | **VERB** | Subject | Rest |
| | Heute | **lerne** | ich | Deutsch. |
| **Yes/No Question** | **VERB** | Subject | (Object) | Rest |
| | **Lernst** | du | Deutsch | heute? |
| **W-Question** | W-Word | **VERB** | Subject | Rest |
| | Wann | **lernst** | du | Deutsch? |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Putting the verb at the end in main clauses ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich Deutsch **lerne**. | Ich **lerne** Deutsch. |
| Er nach Berlin **fährt**. | Er **fährt** nach Berlin. |

### Mistake 2: Keeping subject before verb when starting with time ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Heute **ich lerne** Deutsch. | Heute **lerne ich** Deutsch. |
| Morgen **mein Bruder kommt**. | Morgen **kommt mein Bruder**. |

### Mistake 3: Forgetting verb in position 2 in questions ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wann du **kommst**? | Wann **kommst** du? |
| Wo du **wohnst**? | Wo **wohnst** du? |

### Mistake 4: Putting time after place ❌
| Wrong | Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich fahre nach Berlin **morgen**. | Ich fahre **morgen** nach Berlin. |
| Wir gehen ins Kino **heute Abend**. | Wir gehen **heute Abend** ins Kino. |
