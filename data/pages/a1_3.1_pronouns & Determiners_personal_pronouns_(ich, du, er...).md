# Pronouns: Personal Pronouns (Nominative and Accusative) - A1

Personal pronouns (*Personalpronomen*) replace nouns and refer to people or things. They are essential for speaking naturally without repeating names all the time.

## Why this matters
- You use them constantly in every conversation
- They change depending on the **case** (Nominative vs. Accusative)
- They affect verb conjugation (ich **lerne**, du **lernst**)
- Essential for understanding who is doing what to whom

---

## Part 1: Pronouns in Nominative Case (The Subject)

The **nominative case** is used for the **subject** of the sentence – the person or thing doing the action .

| Person | English | German | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st person singular** | I | **ich** | **Ich** lerne Deutsch. |
| **2nd person singular** | you (informal) | **du** | **Du** bist mein Freund. |
| **3rd person singular** | he | **er** | **Er** kommt aus Berlin. |
| | she | **sie** | **Sie** heißt Maria. |
| | it | **es** | **Es** ist kalt heute. |
| **1st person plural** | we | **wir** | **Wir** gehen ins Kino. |
| **2nd person plural** | you guys (informal) | **ihr** | **Ihr** seid nett. |
| **3rd person plural** | they | **sie** | **Sie** wohnen in Köln. |
| **Formal** | you (formal) | **Sie** | **Sie** sind Herr Müller? |

### Important Notes:
- **sie** (she) and **sie** (they) are the same word! Context tells you which is which.
- **Sie** (formal) is always capitalized and uses the same verb form as sie (they) – plural.
- **es** is used for neuter nouns (das Kind → **es**), but also for weather, time, and impersonal expressions .

### Examples:
- **Ich** bin Anna. (I am Anna.)
- **Du** heißt Tom. (You are called Tom.)
- **Er** ist mein Bruder. (He is my brother.)
- **Sie** ist meine Schwester. (She is my sister.)
- **Es** regnet. (It's raining.)
- **Wir** lernen zusammen. (We learn together.)
- **Ihr** spielt Fußball. (You guys play soccer.)
- **Sie** kommen aus Spanien. (They come from Spain.)
- **Sie** (formal), kommen Sie aus Spanien? (Do you come from Spain?)

## Part 2: Pronouns in Accusative Case (The Direct Object)

The **accusative case** is used for the **direct object** – the person or thing directly affected by the action .

| Nominative (Subject) | Accusative (Direct Object) | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **mich** | me | Er sieht **mich**. |
| **du** | **dich** | you (informal) | Ich liebe **dich**. |
| **er** | **ihn** | him | Ich kenne **ihn**. |
| **sie** (she) | **sie** | her | Ich mag **sie**. |
| **es** | **es** | it | Ich habe **es**. |
| **wir** | **uns** | us | Er besucht **uns**. |
| **ihr** | **euch** | you guys | Ich helfe **euch**. |
| **sie** (they) | **sie** | them | Ich sehe **sie**. |
| **Sie** (formal) | **Sie** | you (formal) | Ich kenne **Sie**. |

### Key Observations:
- **mich** and **dich** are completely different from their nominative forms
- **ihn** (him) is different from **er** (he)
- **sie** (she), **sie** (they), and **Sie** (formal) stay the same in accusative
- **es**, **wir**, **uns**, **ihr**, **euch** also have distinct forms

### Examples with Accusative Pronouns:
| Sentence | Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ich liebe **dich**. | I love you. |
| Siehst du **mich**? | Do you see me? |
| Ich kenne **ihn** nicht. | I don't know him. |
| Besuchst du **sie**? | Are you visiting her? |
| Ich habe **es** gekauft. | I bought it. |
| Er hilft **uns**. | He helps us. |
| Ich rufe **euch** später an. | I'll call you guys later. |
| Kennst du **sie**? | Do you know them? |
| Darf ich **Sie** etwas fragen? | May I ask you something? (formal) |

## Part 3: Common Verbs with Accusative

These common A1 verbs are always followed by the accusative case :

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **lieben** | to love | Ich liebe **dich**. |
| **kennen** | to know (someone) | Kennst du **ihn**? |
| **sehen** | to see | Ich sehe **sie**. (her/them) |
| **hören** | to hear | Hörst du **mich**? |
| **besuchen** | to visit | Wir besuchen **euch**. |
| **fragen** | to ask | Frag **sie**! (her/them) |
| **verstehen** | to understand | Verstehst du **mich**? |
| **treffen** | to meet | Ich treffe **ihn** morgen. |
| **anrufen** | to call | Ruf **mich** an! |
| **einladen** | to invite | Ich lade **dich** ein. |
| **helfen** | to help | Wait – **helfen** takes DATIVE! |
| **helfen** is dative: Ich helfe **dir**. (Not accusative) |

**Important:** Some verbs take the dative case, not accusative! For A1, the most important dative verb is:
- **helfen** (to help) → Ich helfe **dir**. (I help you)
- **danken** (to thank) → Ich danke **Ihnen**. (I thank you – formal)
- **gefallen** (to be pleasing) → Du gefällst **mir**. (I like you – literally "you please me")

## Part 4: Word Order with Pronouns

### Rule 1: Pronouns come before nouns
When both the direct and indirect objects are nouns, the **dative** comes before **accusative** . But with pronouns, the rule changes.

| Order | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Noun – Noun | Ich gebe **dem Mann das Buch**. | I give the man the book. |
| Pronoun – Noun | Ich gebe **ihm das Buch**. | I give him the book. |
| Noun – Pronoun | Ich gebe **dem Mann es**. | I give the man it. (less common) |
| Pronoun – Pronoun | Ich gebe **es ihm**. | I give it to him. |

**Key Rule:** If both objects are pronouns, the **accusative** comes before **dative** .

### Examples:
- Ich schenke **ihr** die Blumen. (I give her the flowers – pronoun + noun)
- Ich schenke **sie ihr**. (I give them to her – accusative + dative)

## Part 5: Using Pronouns with Prepositions

Some common prepositions are always followed by the **accusative** case. When you use a personal pronoun after these prepositions, it must be in the accusative form .

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **für** | for | Das ist Geschenk **für dich**. |
| **ohne** | without | Ich komme **ohne ihn**. |
| **gegen** | against | Das ist **gegen mich**. |
| **um** | around | Alle sitzen **um sie**. (her) |

### Examples:
- Das ist **für mich**? (Is that for me?)
- Ich kann **ohne dich** nicht leben. (I can't live without you.)
- Er ist **gegen uns**. (He is against us.)
- Die Kinder spielen **um ihn** herum. (The children play around him.)

## Part 6: The Formal "Sie"

German has a formal way to address people: **Sie** (always capitalized). It uses the same verb form as **sie** (they) – plural.

| Situation | Informal | Formal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Addressing one person | **du** (you) | **Sie** (you – formal) |
| Addressing multiple people | **ihr** (you guys) | **Sie** (you – formal) |

### Examples:
- **Du** heißt Thomas? (Are you Thomas? – to a friend)
- **Sie** heißen Thomas? (Are you Thomas? – to a stranger/boss)
- Kommt **ihr** aus Berlin? (Do you guys come from Berlin?)
- Kommen **Sie** aus Berlin? (Do you come from Berlin? – formal)

### When to use Sie (formal):
- With strangers
- With authority figures (police, doctors, professors)
- In professional settings
- With elderly people
- When in doubt, use Sie! It's better to be too formal than too casual
